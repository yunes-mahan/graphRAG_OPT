## §3 Where the rewrite cost sits; greedy vs. optimal matching (paper §6)
**Where the cost sits.** At τ=0.9, re-indexing with matching rewrites {{BtTopEg}} of the top-level report tokens (Wikidata {{BtTopWd}}) but {{BtDeepMin}}–{{BtDeepMax}} at levels 2–4, and {{BtBigEg}} of the tokens of communities with more than 200 members against {{BtSmallEg}} for those with at most ten. Part of the fine-level cost comes from the rule rather than from Leiden: swapping a single member of a ten-member community gives J=9/11≈0.82 and forces a rewrite. Optimal (Hungarian) instead of greedy matching changes the cost by {{BtHungNine}} at τ=0.9 (pairs with J≥0.9 are unique best matches), at most {{BtHungMid}} at τ=0.7/0.5 and {{BtHungNever}} with "never".

## §4 Refreshing under a token budget (paper §6)
**Refreshing under a token budget.** With a fixed budget of 10% of a re-index per update, refreshing the lowest-Jaccard reports first leaves the least structural staleness (QA stream {{RsQaTenJac}} vs. {{RsQaTenRr}} for round-robin), which is close to built into the metric.

## §5 Sensitivity to the maximum cluster size (paper §6)
**Sensitivity to the cluster size.** With maximum cluster sizes of 25 and 50 instead of GraphRAG's default 10, the re-cluster split explains {{SensSplitMin}}–{{SensSplitMax}} of the cost variance, τ at least {{SensTauMin}} within re-clustering, and deleting 0.1% of the edges rewrites {{SensDropMin}}–{{SensDropMax}}: larger clusters make re-clustering costlier and less stable, not cheaper.

## §6 Structural scores vs. recall (paper §8)
**Structural scores: no evidence either way.** The 16 QA-stream stores have only {{ValQDistinct}} distinct modularity values, and the score falls with every refresh by construction; its rank correlations with recall are not significant (ρ={{ValRhoQ}} for modularity, ρ={{ValRhoScore}} for the score). We therefore report cost, coverage and answers separately.

## §7 Local questions in full (paper §8)
**Local questions: non-inferior.** Recall ranges from {{QARecallMin}} to {{QARecallMax}} across the 16 stores against {{QARecallFresh}} for fresh reports (Table (paper), Fig. (paper)a), while update input tokens range from {{QATokMin}} to {{QATokMax}}; no store is significantly worse after Holm correction. Because non-significance is not evidence of equality, we ran two one-sided tests (TOST) with a margin of ±{{ValMargin}} recall, about {{MarginShare}} of the fresh-report recall; the margin was set after the first 120 results and before the 180 further questions were generated (file times in the artifact). With {{QANQ}} questions, {{ValNEq}} of {{ValNStores}} stores are equivalent to fresh (all 90% intervals within [{{ValCiLo}},{{ValCiHi}}]). With {{MoreNQ}} questions for six key stores, all {{MoreNStores}} maintained stores are non-inferior (intervals within [{{MoreCiLo}},{{MoreCiHi}}]), {{MoreNEq}} are equivalent, and the two hybrid stores score higher than fresh. This gain is not due to staleness: fresh reports of each maintainer's *own* final partition already differ from the recomputed reference (hybrid {{OwnHyb}} vs. {{OwnFresh}}, p={{OwnHybVsFreshP}}), and against its own-partition reference, which isolates staleness, every maintainer's τ=0.9 store is equivalent ({{OwnNEq}} of 4; intervals within [{{OwnCiLo}},{{OwnCiHi}}]). On broad questions, the partition thus affected recall more than the age of the reports. Three further checks are consistent with this: a second judge (Qwen3-14B; {{QwNEq}} of {{ValNStores}} equivalent, weak agreement with the first judge, ρ={{QwLocalRho}}), a second answering model (Qwen3-14B reading the same reports; all {{QrN}} maintained stores equivalent at lower recall, {{QrMin}}–{{QrMax}}), and a fully open replication (Qwen3-14B writes all reports of both sides and {{CtwoNQ}} questions from the same prompt and seed papers, answers and marks key points): all {{CtwoNStores}} maintained stores at τ=0.9 are non-inferior (intervals within [{{CtwoCiLo}},{{CtwoCiHi}}], {{CtwoNEq}} equivalent; recall {{CtwoRecMin}}–{{CtwoRecMax}}, fresh {{CtwoFresh}}). The six stores of the 300-question test were the four maintainers at τ=0.9 and the never-refreshed recompute and hybrid stores, chosen without a rule fixed beforehand; in the open-model pipeline with {{CthNQ}} questions, the two never-refreshed stores not in the 300-question test, warm-started and frontier-restricted Leiden, are also equivalent to fresh reports (Δ={{CthDiff}}, intervals within [{{CthCiLo}},{{CthCiHi}}]). With a stricter margin of ±{{TmMargin}} ({{TmShare}} of the fresh recall), all {{TmMoreN}} stores of the 300-question test, all {{TmOwnN}} own-partition comparisons and both {{CthNQ}}-question open-model stores stay non-inferior (lowest bounds {{TmMoreLo}}, {{TmOwnLo}}, {{TmCthLo}}); in the smaller open replication, {{TmCtwoNNi}} of {{TmCtwoN}} do (warm start: {{TmCtwoFailLo}}).

## §8 Global questions in full (paper §8)
**Global questions: no store worse, equivalence not established.** Both global tests use the same pairwise judge (gpt-5.6-terra, four criteria, both presentation orders); a store answers from all its reports on the QA stream and by GraphRAG-style map–reduce over its reports on the entity graph. On the QA stream, maintained, never-refreshed and title-only report stores obtain win rates between {{GlobStaleMin}} and {{GlobStaleMax}} against fresh reports (smallest p={{GlobStaleMinP}}); on the flat entity-graph stores none is significantly worse after correction (smallest uncorrected p={{EgQaMinP}} over {{EgQaTests}} tests). With {{GlobNQ}} questions, however, equivalence within [0.4,0.6] is established for only {{ValGlobEq}} of {{ValGlobN}} store–criterion pairs, so differences of about 0.1 in win rate cannot be excluded.

## §9 Entity graph at levels 0–2 with the open model (paper §8)
**Entity graph, GraphRAG's levels 0–2 (open model).** On the LLM-extracted entity graph we compared the maintained index (warm-started hierarchical Leiden with identifier matching, τ=0.9) with a fresh re-index at GraphRAG's default levels 0–2 ({{ConeComms}} communities in the fresh index), fully with Qwen3-14B: {{ConeNQ}} distinct local questions were generated from recent documents and their seven most similar documents with the prompt above; each store retrieves the six communities whose report input (member entities and their relationships) is most similar to the question, so that only retrieved communities need a report ({{ConeReports}} reports written lazily, each from the member set at its last rewrite). This retrieval is our own setup (top six similar communities, reports written lazily), not GraphRAG's local search. Only {{ConeStale}} of the retrieved maintained reports were stale, so this test mainly measures the partition, not staleness: the warm-start partition loses a little (Δ={{ConeDiff}}, Wilcoxon p={{ConeP}}, 90% interval [{{ConeCiLo}},{{ConeCiHi}}]; recall {{ConeWarm}} vs. {{ConeFresh}}), and non-inferiority within ±{{ValMargin}} is not established on this graph.

## §10 Targeted staleness and coverage probes (paper §8)
**Targeted probes: where staleness shows.** Broad questions may not touch what changed; we built two probes ({{ProbeStaleN}} questions each, same pipeline). *Staleness probe:* for the four never-refreshed stores, 25 reports per maintainer whose community has since gained at least four papers, with questions supported only by the papers that joined after the report was written, answered by the stale store and by fresh reports of the same partition: stale reports reach recall {{ProbeStaleMine}} against {{ProbeStaleRef}} (Δ={{ProbeStaleDiff}}, p{{ProbeStaleP}}; significant for {{ProbeStaleSigHolm}} of 4 maintainers after Holm correction). This is a worst case by construction; the expected loss is about {{ProbeStaleLoss}} times the share of questions about recent content. *Coverage probe:* for GraphRAG's update and Zep-style attachment, questions from documents whose entities lie mostly outside every top-level community are answered from the top-level reports: recall {{ProbeCovMine}} against {{ProbeCovRef}} for the fresh re-index (Δ={{ProbeCovDiff}}, p{{ProbeCovP}} pooled; neither rule is significant alone).

## §11 Reports vs. vector retrieval (paper §8)
**Summaries serve global questions.** For local questions, plain vector retrieval of 12 papers reaches recall {{LocVec}} against {{LocSums}} for fresh reports (p{{LocVecP}}), also with the same retriever for both. For global questions the picture reverses: vector retrieval wins only {{GlobVecComp}} of the comprehensiveness comparisons ({{GlobVecMatchComp}} with length-matched context), and reading only the reports closest to the question loses against reading all of them. In the flat entity-graph experiment, GraphRAG's delta store holds {{EgGdReports}} reports instead of {{EgFreshReports}}, so every global question reads {{EgGdQueryX}}× the measured report tokens (its reports are slightly shorter, so less than the report ratio) and its update savings are used up after {{EgBreakEven}} questions.

 A gain on the score is expected by construction (the price comes from it). On held-out orders, rollback improves warm-started Leiden on {{PrWarmWins}} of {{PrWarmN}} orders (mean {{PrWarmDiff}}, p<{{PrWarmP}}; it is slightly worse on collab-10) and frontier-restricted Leiden on {{PrFrontWins}} of {{PrFrontN}} (p<{{PrFrontP}}), and it improves both on all five orders of the Wikidata and entity graphs and on ogbl-citation2 (Table (paper)), at {{ScaleOverheadWarm}} running time over warm-started Leiden. ## §13 Recomputation and appending (paper §7)
**Recomputation and appending score low.** Recomputing Leiden after every update rewrites the reports of communities that hold {{RPArxFRecomp}} of the nodes on both main streams, and GraphRAG's delta update fragments its partition as the graph grows (mean modularity {{GdQArx}} on arXiv-15 against {{WarmQArx}} for warm-started Leiden); both are below the warm-started, frontier-restricted and lazy maintainers in Table (paper).

## §15 Deletion and lineage in full (paper §9)
Deleting a document (retraction, erasure request) must also invalidate every report derived from it. We deleted a random 1% or 5% of the documents of the entity graph (3 draws each), removed the entities and relationships extracted only from them, and maintained GraphRAG's hierarchical stores (Section (paper)) as their rules prescribe: re-clustering maintainers re-cluster and apply the τ=0.9 rule; GraphRAG's update has no deletion path and Zep-style attachment no removal step, so they keep all reports. A report *leaks* if it is still served and was written for a member set that contains a removed entity, or both endpoints of a removed relationship. With 5% of the documents deleted, {{DelGuFive}} of GraphRAG's served reports leak and {{DelZepFive}} of the Zep-style reports (1%: {{DelGuOne}} and {{DelZepOne}}); many leaking reports still name the deleted entity in their text (top level: {{DelTxtGuFive}} and {{DelTxtZepFive}}). Re-clustering maintainers leak only {{DelReFive}}, but only because their instability rewrites {{DelReCostFive}} of the report tokens anyway. These rates are lower bounds: facts from deleted documents can also survive in reports through surviving entities whose descriptions drew on them. Lightfoot  deletes documents exactly from a provenance-tracked graph without community reports, and RelGuard  perturbs or removes relations before indexing for differential privacy; neither maintains reports. Lineage-based invalidation, as proposed for agent memory by Agentic Unlearning , removes the leaks: rewriting every served report that contains a removed entity or relationship costs {{DelLinGuFive}} (GraphRAG's update) and {{DelLinZepFive}} (Zep-style) of the tokens of a full re-index, and conservative document-level lineage, which rewrites every report containing an entity mentioned in a deleted document, costs {{DelDocGuFive}} and {{DelDocZepFive}}. Deletion is thus the case where a staleness tolerance is not acceptable.

## §16 Topic-anchored report reuse in full (paper §10)
**Topic-anchored report reuse.** Every report is stored with a topic fingerprint, the mean of its members' mean-centred text embeddings; a community with at least five members reuses the stored report with the most similar fingerprint if the cosine is at least θ=0.95, across identifiers, splits and merges. On the QA stream (Table (paper)) it cuts the reports written for recomputed Leiden from {{IdRecompWritten}} to {{TopicRecompWritten}} ({{TopicRecompShare}} of the tokens) without a significant loss in local recall. The global judge even prefers it to fresh reports (comprehensiveness {{GlobTopicComp}}, p={{GlobTopicP}}), which we do not read as a gain: the judge rates breadth, not currency; the effect does not survive Holm correction over the {{GlobNTests}} tests of the reuse study (p={{GlobTopicPHolm}}); {{GlobNAboveHalf}} of the {{GlobNStoresReuse}} reuse stores score above 0.5 against the same fresh answers, a shared tilt rather than an effect of reuse; and answer length does not explain it ({{GlobTopicWords}} vs. {{GlobFreshWords}} words). The rule is lenient: a control that replaces members at random so that J=0.7 still passes θ in {{CosRandSeven}} of the communities, so the fingerprint acts largely as a looser overlap rule, and the judge rates {{TopicSubOtherMin}}–{{TopicSubOtherMax}} of the reports reused for a different member set as substantively different from a fresh report (Table (paper)). Combined with rollback and title-only inputs, update tokens fell to {{PipeShare}} of recomputation with local recall {{PipeRecall}}.

## §19 Consensus and k-core hierarchies in full (paper §6)
**Reproducibility is not stability.** Hossain and Sarıyüce  explain this instability: on sparse knowledge graphs, modularity admits exponentially many near-optimal partitions. Their remedy, a deterministic k-core hierarchy, makes communities *reproducible* (same input, same output). Maintenance needs *stability* (similar input, similar output), and GraphRAG's clustering is already reproducible through its fixed seed. Table (paper) compares remedies. Our first stand-in for a k-core hierarchy (BFS splits to GraphRAG's size bound) is reproducible but even less stable ({{SvKcoreDrop}} rewritten after deleting 0.1% of the edges), with almost no modularity; the authors' code  behaves very differently. Its three size-bounded heuristics (RkH, M2hC, MRC; at most 20 entities per community, fixed by the authors) rewrite only {{KcADropMin}}–{{KcADropMax}} of the report tokens after deleting 0.1% of the edges (Wikidata {{KcAWdDropMin}}–{{KcAWdDropMax}}) and {{KcALastMin}}–{{KcALastMax}} for the last update (Wikidata {{KcAWdLastMin}}–{{KcAWdLastMax}}); over the full update streams with identifier matching, RkH rewrites {{KcAStreamRkH}} (Wikidata {{KcAWdStreamRkH}}) of a GraphRAG re-index per update; with GraphRAG's size bound of 10 instead of the authors' 20 (our variant), RkH still rewrites only {{KtenDropEg}} (Wikidata {{KtenDropWd}}) after the deletion. It is therefore both reproducible and stable, but, like consensus clustering, at the price of the coarse levels: its top level is a single community (the whole connected component), the next level already holds {{KcASecMin}}–{{KcASecMax}} communities (Wikidata {{KcAWdSecMin}}–{{KcAWdSecMax}}), and its leaf modularity is lower than Leiden's ({{KcALeafMin}}–{{KcALeafMax}} vs. {{SvLeidenLeaf}}; Wikidata {{KcAWdLeafMin}}–{{KcAWdLeafMax}} vs. {{SvWdLeidenLeaf}}). Warm-starting from the leaves of the unperturbed clustering does not help ({{SvWarmDrop}}). Our thresholded consensus variant (after Lancichinetti and Fortunato ) weights every edge by how often 100 Leiden runs put its endpoints into the same leaf, drops edges below 0.5 agreement, reattaches entities that lose all their edges to the community of their most-agreeing neighbour, and runs GraphRAG's hierarchical Leiden on the weighted graph. It looks very stable ({{FxThDrop}} rewritten on the entity graph, {{FxWdThDrop}} on Wikidata; per update over the full streams {{FxThStream}} and {{FxWdThStream}} of a re-index, against {{FxLdStream}} and {{FxWdLdStream}}), but it achieves this by removing the hierarchy: the thresholded graph falls apart into thousands of components, which hierarchical Leiden cannot merge, so only {{FxThLevels}} levels remain and the top level has {{FxThTop}} communities (Wikidata {{FxWdThTop}}) instead of {{FxLdTop}} ({{FxWdLdTop}}). A global query over the top level would read thousands of reports. Two variants that keep more of the hierarchy are less stable (Table (paper)): *soft consensus* (agreement as edge weight) and *consensus leaves plus Leiden*, which rewrites little or nothing less than GraphRAG. The more of the coarse hierarchy a clustering keeps, the less stable it is: the instability lives in the coarse levels, and both consensus and the k-core hierarchy make an index stable by flattening them. It therefore suits flat or leaf-level use, not GraphRAG's overview queries over the top levels; an open-model test on the {{GlobNQ}} global questions was inconclusive: at GraphRAG's top-level budget of {{CqBudget}} reports (for the k-core hierarchy, whose top level is one community, its {{CqBudget}} largest second-level communities, a selection of ours: the authors answer global questions from all communities of the leaf or its parent level), the consensus and k-core reports cover only {{CqCovCons}} and {{CqCovKcl}} of the entities, but the pairwise Qwen3-14B judge preferred the second answer in {{CqSecondMin}}–{{CqSecondMax}} of its verdicts, so its win rates ({{CqWinMin}}–{{CqWinMax}}) carry no information.
